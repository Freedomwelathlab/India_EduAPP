## 1. Summary

1. **The status quo is the biggest competitor.** Registers, Excel, WhatsApp groups and printed question banks (Educart, Oswaal and similar) are how most schools run periodic tests and IA today [I, to be tested in interviews]. Vidya has to beat "free and familiar" on teacher time saved.
2. **LEAD Group is the strongest direct competitor.** It has about **8,500 partner schools**, targets 1,200 more in 2026 and 25,000 by 2030 [C, S17, S18]. FY25 revenue was **₹367 Cr with operating EBITDA breakeven** [C, S19]. It is building AI products (Ms Curie, Fluento) [F, S20] and is reported to be considering an IPO around FY28 [F, S17]. About 90% of its schools are outside metros [C, S17].
3. **The content-plus-smart-class incumbents claim large footprints:** Next Education (18,000+ schools, bootstrapped) [C, S21] and Extramarks (15,000+ schools, quote-only pricing) [C, S16, S22]. They sell content and hardware-led "smart class" packages. Assessment is one module among many.
4. **The market is consolidating around hardware and ERP.** Teachmint is now a hardware-led business: it reports being India's #1 interactive-panel brand in H1 FY26 and FY26 revenue of about ₹205 Cr [C/U, S23, S24]. Entab (CampusCare ERP, 2,500+ schools [C]) was **acquired by Nelnet on 5 Oct 2026** [F, S25]. [I] Both are better as **integration partners** (panel display, ERP roster/marks sync) than as head-on competitors.
5. **The big B2C players are not credible school partners right now.** BYJU'S is in insolvency, with bidding paused by the NCLT [F, S26, S27]. PhysicsWallah entered schools in early 2026, then reversed to "100% asset-light" with about 50 partner schools [F, S28, S29]. [I] Schools burned by BYJU'S-era sales are wary, which helps a trust-first, compliance-first pitch.
6. **Government content is free and expected.** CBSE points schools to DIKSHA for Class 6–10 practice questions [F, S30]. [I] Vidya should **link to DIKSHA** rather than compete with free official content, and win on workflow (IA, remediation, analytics).

## 4. White space and what it means for the wedge

**White space [I]:** a **lightweight, school-centric assessment and remediation layer** that:
1. keeps the school's NCERT books, teachers and existing ERP/panels (no curriculum swap, unlike a full bundle);
2. is **native to the 2026-27 CBSE rules**: best-2-of-3 periodic tests, the four-part IA register, Standard/Advanced, two Class 10 board attempts, formative-only topics;
3. closes the loop from diagnosis to practice to teacher-led remediation, with explainable reasons;
4. is **trust-first under DPDP**: the school owns the data, no ads, data in India, no Aadhaar/APAAR.

**Strategy implications [R]:**
| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Position against **LEAD** as "keep your books and your way of teaching; add the assessment and remediation layer in a day" | LEAD's strength is the whole-system bundle; some schools don't want to replace their curriculum [H, test in interviews] |
| Make **IA register + PT automation** the opening demo, not content | Content is crowded and free (DIKSHA, Extramarks, books); IA admin is under-served [H] |
| Build **integrations, not rivalries**, with panels (Teachmint) and ERPs (Entab/Nelnet, others): CSV first, APIs later | Schools already own these; lowers switching cost |
| Expect **price pressure**: the 2021 Embibe school price (~₹500/child/yr) is a low anchor in budget schools | Validate willingness to pay in the 20 interviews before setting prices |
| Use BYJU'S-era distrust: transparent pricing, no student upsell, school-billed only | Trust is a differentiator while the sector's reputation is damaged |

**Interview questions to add (feeds C6):** "Have you evaluated LEAD, Extramarks or Next Education? What made you choose or reject them?" and "Do you already have interactive panels or an ERP? Which?"
